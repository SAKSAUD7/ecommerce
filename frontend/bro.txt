 Yes. If your goal is to **build your own ecommerce admin portal comparable to Shopify**, I would not limit it to “Products + Orders + Customers.” A serious platform needs the entire commerce operating system: catalog, inventory, orders, payments, fulfillment, returns, customers, marketing, storefront/CMS, analytics, finance, tax, shipping, integrations, automation, security, and eventually B2B/multi-store.

I went through Shopify's current admin structure and documentation, including its Products, Orders, Customers, Analytics, Growth/Marketing, Discounts, Content, inventory, B2B, checkout, permissions and app-extension capabilities. Shopify's own admin describes Orders, Products, Customers, Analytics, marketing/promotions and Discounts as core areas, while its current 2026 changelog shows that the terminology and feature set continue evolving—for example, Marketing was renamed **Growth** in June 2026. ([Shopify Help Center][1])

Below is the **master blueprint I would use for your ecommerce admin portal**.

## ⭐ The biggest difference I would make

If you're actually planning to build this as your own **Shopify-style SaaS**, I would make **Dropshipping Operations + Profit Analytics + Supplier Intelligence** the differentiator.

A normal ecommerce platform says:

> **"You sold 1,000 products."**

Your platform should say:

> **"You sold 1,000 products, Supplier A fulfilled 620, Supplier B fulfilled 380, Supplier B is 18% slower, Product X generated the highest contribution margin, Campaign Y generated revenue but was unprofitable after supplier/shipping/ad costs, and Product Z should be paused because its supplier price increased and its margin fell below your threshold."**

That requires an underlying **event/data architecture**, not just dashboard charts.

### The core data flow should therefore be:

```text
                    TRAFFIC
                       ↓
                 STOREFRONT
                       ↓
                    PRODUCT
                       ↓
                     CART
                       ↓
                   CHECKOUT
                       ↓
                    PAYMENT
                       ↓
                    ORDER
                       ↓
              ┌────────┴────────┐
              ↓                 ↓
         CUSTOMER           SUPPLIER
                                ↓
                         ORDER ROUTING
                                ↓
                           FULFILLMENT
                                ↓
                            SHIPMENT
                                ↓
                            DELIVERY
                                ↓
                         RETURN/REFUND
                                ↓
                         FINAL PROFIT
```

And **every event** should feed your analytics engine.

This is important because Shopify's current analytics already provides unified dashboards/reports, customizable metric cards, reports, data exploration and automated insights; its inventory reporting covers sell-through, inventory value and adjustments, while customer reporting covers new/returning customers and purchase behavior. ([Shopify Help Center][10])

**If you're building this with the intention of eventually selling it to other businesses, the next document should be the actual technical master specification:** `Module → Page → Tab → Component → Field → Button → Action → API → Database table → Permission → Status → Automation → Analytics event → Report`. That would turn everything above into an actual buildable blueprint rather than just a feature list.
